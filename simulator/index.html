      Event log

        How it detects

        1. Baseline. The first three seconds set a reference level for every frequency bin. Steady hum, fan tones and broadcast carriers become part of it.
        2. Threshold. A bin counts as active when it stays above its baseline by the sensitivity setting for two frames in a row.
        3. Keying. A bin that switches on and off several times in a short window is keyed. Steady new tones are listed but do not raise an alert. The fan detector uses a longer window because fans change speed slowly.
        4. Pairing and linking. Two keyed bins that take turns are reported as FSK. Several keyed bins that switch together are reported as linked lines, the pattern of a display cable's harmonics.
        5. Burst watch. Disk seeks are not narrowband. A separate detector tracks total energy in 1.5–6 kHz and counts bursts.
        6. Impulse rejection. If a quarter of all bins jump at once, the frame is treated as a click and held at its previous state.

        Techniques simulated

        Ultrasonic FSK
        Two tones near 19 kHz, above most adult hearing.
        Fan speed
        A fan tone that shifts between two speeds 160 Hz apart. Slow, a few bits per second.
        Disk seeks
        Short mechanical bursts in 1.5–6 kHz. A burst is a 1, silence is a 0.
        Keyed carrier
        One narrow RF line switched on and off.
        Video cable
        Three lines 25 MHz apart that switch together, like pixel-clock harmonics.

        Signals here are synthetic and simplified. Thresholds need retuning against real microphone and SDR captures. A channel already running during calibration enters the baseline and can be missed; use Recalibrate to try it.
